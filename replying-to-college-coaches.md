# How to reply to a college coach's email

A coach wrote back. Now what? This page has a short, copy-paste reply for the eight kinds of coach emails athletes commonly get, plus a few rules that apply to all of them. Every sport, plain text, no sign-up.

Still need to send the first email? Start with our [templates for your sport](https://judestrahm.github.io/college-coach-email-templates/) or our free [College Coach Email Generator](https://sentry-sell-recruit.judestrahm.workers.dev/).

## Rules for every reply

1. **Reply yourself, from your own email.** Coaches want to hear from the athlete. A parent can be copied.
2. **Answer within a day if you can.** A quick, short reply beats a perfect one sent next week.
3. **Answer exactly what they asked first.** If they asked for a schedule, the schedule goes in line one.
4. **Keep the subject line.** Hit reply so the coach sees the whole thread, and keep your grad year and position in it.
5. **Sign off with the same block every time:** name, grad year, position or events, high school, phone, film or results link.
6. **Check that it's real.** Before you pay for a camp or send personal details, make sure the coach's name and email match the school's athletics staff page.
7. **Track it.** Write down the school, the coach, the date and what you promised to send, then do it.

Contact rules differ by division and sport, so how and when a coach can reply varies. If you are unsure, check the current NCAA, NAIA or NJCAA recruiting rules for your sport or ask your high school coach.

## 1. The coach asks a question or wants more

Example: "Thanks for reaching out. Send me your schedule." or "What's your 40 time?"

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you for getting back to me. Here is my [season] schedule:

[date] vs [opponent], [time], [location]
[date] at [opponent], [time], [location]

My updated [film/results]: [link]

I'll send you my results after the [event/game] on [date].

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## 2. The coach sends a recruiting questionnaire

Fill it out the same day, then reply so the coach knows it is done.

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you for sending the questionnaire. I completed it today ([date]).

Since my first email I have [one new thing: new PR, new film, honor, updated GPA].

I'm very interested in [School] and would like to know what the next step is for a [grad year] [position].

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## 3. The coach invites you to a camp or clinic

Many camp emails go to long lists. Before you pay, reply and ask whether the staff wants to evaluate you there.

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you for the invitation to your [camp name] on [date].

Before I register, would your staff be evaluating [grad year] [position]s at this camp, and is there anything you would want to see from me there?

My latest [film/results]: [link]

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## 4. "We'll keep following you" or "not right now"

This is not a final no. Thank them, then give them a reason to look again later.

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you for the honest answer. I'll keep working and send you an update after [season/event] with new film and results.

Is there anything specific you would want to see improve before then?

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

Then put a date on your calendar and actually send that update.

## 5. The coach asks for a transcript or test scores

```text
Coach Daniels,

Here is my unofficial transcript (attached). My current GPA is [GPA] [weighted/unweighted] and my [SAT/ACT] score is [score, or "I take the SAT on (date)"].

I plan to study [major or area] and would like to know more about it at [School].

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

Send only what they asked for. Before you attach a transcript, cover any Social Security number on it, and never type your SSN in an email. Your NCAA Eligibility Center ID is fine to share if a coach asks for it.

## 6. The coach invites you to call or visit

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you, I would really like that. I'm free [two or three specific days and times, with your time zone].

My [mom/dad/guardian], [name], may join us. Their number is [phone].

Thank you,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## 7. The coach asks for updated film or results

```text
Coach Daniels,

Here is my updated [film/results/times] link: [link]

New since last time: [one or two lines, e.g. "first two clips are from our playoff game on (date)" or "new PR of (mark) at (meet) on (date)"].

Thank you for taking another look,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## 8. The coach says it is not a fit

A kind, short reply keeps the door open, and coaches often know each other.

```text
Coach Daniels,

Thank you for letting me know and for taking the time to look at my [film/results]. I appreciate it.

If you know a program where a [grad year] [position] like me might fit, I'd be grateful for the suggestion.

Best of luck this season,
[Name] | [Grad year] [Position] | [High school, City, ST] | [Phone] | [Film or results link]
```

## What not to do

- Don't leave a coach waiting a week for something they asked for.
- Don't send the same reply to every school without changing the coach's name and the school.
- Don't attach big video files. Send a link.
- Don't let a parent write the reply as if they were the athlete.
